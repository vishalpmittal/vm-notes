# The Four Pillars of Engineering Management

An engineering manager's core job is to **amplify the collective impact of the people they lead.** That job rests on four pillars — and the balance among them constantly shifts with context, with no title change.

## Key Takeaways

- The role has four areas of responsibility: **People, Technical, Product, and Delivery** leadership
- **No pillar is a permanent priority** — a migration favors Technical, rapid growth favors People, a reorg shifts everything. Consciously rebalancing to the current moment *is* the job
- The unifying mandate: *"understand what is preventing your team from succeeding, and remove those obstacles"*
- A team *"can execute flawlessly and still fail to deliver an outcome"* — which is why Product leadership sits alongside Technical and Delivery

## The Four Pillars

**1. People Leadership** — building an environment where individuals do their best work and the team functions as a whole: hiring, onboarding, career growth, culture, coaching, conflict, performance, difficult feedback. The most nuanced pillar (*"people problems are rarely 0 or 1"*); its nature shifts as the team matures.

**2. Technical Leadership** — guiding the team to consistently make good technical decisions aligned with business goals. *Not* being the best engineer or making every call. Includes architecture guidance, challenging assumptions, managing tech debt/standards, and staying hands-on (code, PRs, incidents). Goal: *"helping others become technically sound and better than you."*

**3. Product Leadership** — ensuring the team builds the **right** thing, not just builds it well. Partner with PMs (don't replace them), balance customer value against technical reality, evaluate stakeholder/exec requests, and help decide **what not to build**.

**4. Delivery Leadership** — turning ideas into working software predictably; *"the operating system for software development."* Process-agnostic (Agile/Scrum/Kanban are means, not ends). Focus on removing friction: clarify, surface dependencies, manage risk early, handle shifting priorities, and keep communication flowing as the team grows.

## Actionable Insights

- **Consciously rebalance** the four pillars to the current moment instead of defaulting to the one you're most comfortable with (usually Technical)
- **Don't over-index on elegance** — *"a technically elegant solution is not always the right solution"* if it's too slow or complex
- When a request is too costly (e.g., a 6-month feature), **reduce scope / validate assumptions / ship smaller**
- Treat process as a means: optimize for quality delivery on a reasonable timeline, not ceremony
